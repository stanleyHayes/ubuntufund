# Campaign staff decisions

Implemented September 2026. Focused verification completed; this does not establish legal approval or actual staff inspection.

The current administrator detail read provides a version fingerprint over the complete campaign content, media references, organizer, goal/currency, dates, tier, locked fee and decision revision. Public/owner reads do not receive this staff token. Raised totals are excluded from the version because donations must not invalidate a content review. Status and a monotonically increasing decision revision prevent old approvals from being reused after blocking/reopening.

Approve, reject, block and return-to-review require the displayed version and at least 20 characters of notes. Approval additionally requires explicit acknowledgement of full content/media and fundraising/organizer/beneficiary review. The UI shows the story, beneficiary labels, URL, tier, fee lock, dates and every media reference. The acknowledgement is an operator attestation, not proof of actual identity/media inspection or regulatory permission.

The MongoDB transaction validates current staff role/credential version, prohibits self-review, compares the complete version, updates only status/revision, and records the private decision and audit event. A staff-account write makes concurrent credential/role changes conflict with the decision. Approval checks current owner availability, publishing restrictions, legal acceptance, verification level, account compliance cap, positive GHS goal and campaign expiry. A campaign that already met its goal retains the funded milestone on approval. Staff approval cannot publish a blocked campaign directly: Return to review changes it to pending_review; a new complete review is required.

Matching retries return the current campaign without replaying the mutation or creating duplicate evidence. Different decisions/notes for the same version conflict. A failed audit write rolls back status, revision and decision. Concurrent campaign writes trigger transaction retry and version validation; financial totals are never copied from a stale entity. The generic whole-entity update path is no longer used by staff decisions. Blocking/rejection also ends known active live sessions, removes overlay access and queues provider shutdown in the same transaction. The existing 30-second reconciler retries provider failures; no external provider call occurs inside the financial/audit transaction. Actual provider shutdown remains pending until confirmed.

A private paginated history shows reviewers, dates, notes, status transitions, fingerprints and the reviewed content snapshot. PDF/XLSX/CSV history exports use the existing authorized/branded export flow and every server page. Snapshots hold selected public campaign fields, not KYC documents, payout accounts or passwords. Account-erasure cleanup removes this duplicate public-content snapshot, preserving decision provenance, notes/audit evidence and financial references. It reduces the campaign's private `contentAdmission` record to its non-personal outline in the same way and keeps the `campaign.content_admission` audit entry (retention basis in `PUBLICATION_REVIEWS.md`, "Retention"). Notes can contain personal information: purpose-specific retention, lawful holds, third-party redaction and processor/backup erasure remain in the data-rights operating work. Do not interpret the retained audit as anonymous or full erasure.

The old admin Edit/Delete controls had no matching API routes and have been removed. No hard-delete operation was introduced. General campaign-content editing remains unavailable until it has a complete merged-version review flow. The previously broken unblock request is now a supported, audited return to private review.

New legacy campaigns are not silently treated as content-screened: approval captures staff attestation against the entire actual legacy record. This does not retroactively review campaigns that were already public. Recorded media URLs identify the references reviewed, but remote asset replacement/actual inspection and legacy media rollout still require operational evidence.

## Campaigns waiting for a content check (30 September 2026)

By the owner's decision of 30 September 2026, a new campaign whose content needs a person is created as `pending_review` and decided here, alongside high-goal and on-behalf campaigns. That covers new photos or video, no permission for automated screening, text that screening flagged, and screening that was unavailable (see "Campaign creation" in `PUBLICATION_REVIEWS.md`). There is no separate proposal to approve and the organizer resubmits nothing. The campaign records why it was held (`contentReviewReason`). The Staff decision panel shows that under "Why it is waiting" as a content-check chip, with what to look at, above the story and every media attachment. The review-queue email names the same content check.

The decision itself is unchanged: the displayed version, at least 20 characters of notes, both attestations (complete content and every media attachment; organizer, beneficiary, goal and fundraising requirements), the self-review ban, the current-organizer checks and the transactional decision/audit record all apply. Approval clears the content check (`contentReviewClearedAt`/`By`, audited) and makes the campaign live. The chip shows only while the check is outstanding, so a cleared campaign that returns to review for another reason (a reopen, the beneficiary's acceptance) does not point staff back at content they already approved. Rejection keeps it private, and the organizer is told either way.

A beneficiary change is new public text and is admitted like a new campaign's content (see "Beneficiary changes are new content" in `PUBLICATION_REVIEWS.md`). When screening does not clear it (no permission, a flag, or no screener), the content check reopens with that reason and `contentReviewTrigger: beneficiary_change`; the panel adds that the organizer changed the beneficiary after the campaign was checked, so staff read the new name and reason. Approving clears it and sends the new invitation as for any held invitation. A campaign whose check a change reopened keeps its lifetime verification slot even if the change is rejected.

The self-review ban also covers a campaign whose beneficiary invitation (held or sent, not yet answered) is addressed to the reviewing administrator's account email: before consent no beneficiary is linked, so checking the linked account alone would let them approve the campaign that pays them. The same staff cannot reassign such a campaign or name themselves as its beneficiary.

Staff are emailed whenever a campaign comes back to them, each occasion under its own key: a beneficiary change that reopened the check or that leaves the campaign waiting for staff, a beneficiary named again (by the organizer or a staff reassignment) for a check whose invitation was withdrawn, a beneficiary's acceptance that leaves it in review, and a return to review when staff are the next to act (its content waits with someone to invite once it is cleared, or no consent does). A campaign past its end date, which review refuses, is never announced.

Until approval nothing about the campaign leaves the organizer and staff. Its beneficiary invitation (on-behalf campaigns) is held, never sent, and collaborator invitations are recorded without notifying the invitee. On an on-behalf campaign staff therefore approve before the beneficiary's consent: the approval clears the content and sends the held invitation in the same transaction, and the panel says so. The campaign stays in review until the beneficiary accepts; their acceptance publishes it only where consent alone would have published screened content, otherwise staff approve again after it (and are alerted). Approval also sends the queued collaborator invitation notices, each once, including one saved while the approval was committing.

After a rejection or block, only a new staff approval publishes an on-behalf campaign: blocking switches consent publication off and so does returning it to review, and the beneficiary's acceptance reads the review history, so a campaign blocked (or blocked and reopened) before blocking recorded this follows the same rule without a data migration. Rejecting or blocking held content also withdraws its held invitation and removes the address and its hash; approving it after a return to review then answers 409 until the organizer names the beneficiary again. That return to review sends no staff alert: the organizer's notice asks them to name the beneficiary again, their panels show `name_beneficiary`, the Staff decision panel says no invitation is waiting (not that approving sends one), and the team is alerted once a beneficiary is named. Until a decision, the campaign is excluded from every public read, listing, share preview and donation path. Only the organizer's account and staff can open it (organization team members see its title and status on the team page). The reason stays on the campaign after the decision and is returned to the organizer and staff, and to the campaign's managers once it is public; public, beneficiary and donor reads never return it.

Rejecting or blocking a campaign whose check is outstanding also declines that exact version in Publication reviews (author-visible note; staff notes stay internal), under both the fingerprint rebuilt from the stored campaign and, while the stored version is still the one admitted, the one its admission recorded (a beneficiary change held by the check, or a staff reassignment, removes it), so creating it again, even with automated screening, answers 422 until the record is purged. Approving it after a reopen removes the decline of the version approved; a rejected version replaced by a new beneficiary before the approval stays declined. Such a campaign never went live, so it does not count against the organizer's lifetime verification allowance, nor does one left unreviewed past its end date.
